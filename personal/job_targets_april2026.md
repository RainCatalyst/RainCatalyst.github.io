## General Application Tips

**On the Blue Card conversation:**  
Ask about Blue Card compatibility in your first recruiter call — not as an afterthought, but as a normal logistical question. "I'm an international student finishing my degree in Germany and I'll be applying for a Blue Card — can you confirm this role's salary range is above the €45,934 threshold for shortage occupations?" Any serious English-speaking employer will handle this without blinking.

**On the NDA projects:**  
You have two Godot NDA roles listed on your CV. Lead with whatever you CAN say: the publisher's country, the type of game, the systems you owned. Interviewers understand NDAs — just don't hide behind them entirely.

**On your pivot framing:**  
You don't need to disown gamedev — you need to reframe it. "I've spent 3+ years building real-time interactive systems with strict performance requirements, shipping to PC, Nintendo Switch, and mobile. The core skills — systems design, performance optimization, real-time rendering, cross-functional collaboration — are what I'm bringing to a broader tech context." This framing works for automotive simulation, fintech, enterprise software, and everything in between.

**On the Seniority vs. Experience gap:**  
You have 3–4 years of combined professional + shipped indie experience, which is genuinely mid-level by any reasonable measure. In Germany, "Senior" often starts at 5 years but is negotiable. Apply to senior roles anyway — the worst outcome is rejection, and sometimes they'll invite you for a mid-level conversation.

**On timing:**  
If your graduation is in ~3 months (July/August 2026), start applying now. Many German companies have multi-month hiring timelines (especially large ones like Vodafone/Telekom). The TGR-E and Scalable roles can move faster. Having an offer in place before you graduate means you go straight from student visa to Blue Card without needing a job-seeking visa in between.
